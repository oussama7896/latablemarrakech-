# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

**Primary: Affluent leisure travelers staying in Marrakech riads or villas.**
Couples, families, and friend groups, roughly mid-30s to mid-50s. They are mid-trip-planning, browsing from outside Morocco on a phone or a laptop, in English or French (sometimes Arabic). They have already decided to stay somewhere private; they do not want to leave it for dinner. The chef-at-villa experience is one of three to five anchor moments they are stitching into a trip.

**Secondary: Wedding planners, villa concierges, and DMC operators booking on behalf of those guests.**
Time-pressed, evaluating multiple suppliers, comparing on credibility and feel as much as on price. They forward what they find; the page has to look like something they are proud to recommend.

The job both audiences are hiring the site to do: *Skip the restaurant trek. Have a chef-led Moroccan dinner come to our villa.*

## Product Purpose

Convince an affluent traveler (or the planner who serves them) that the right move on at least one night of their Marrakech trip is to keep the table at home and let a chef-led Moroccan dinner come to them — and make booking it via WhatsApp feel inevitable.

Success looks like:
- More confirmed WhatsApp bookings from existing traffic.
- The site reads as premium enough to match the experience the chef actually delivers.
- It does not look like one more entry in the 30-other-Marrakech-private-chef sea.

## Brand Personality

Three words: **Sensory · Cinematic · Generous.**

Heightened, almost cinematic. The language of a Moroccan dinner that hits every sense: pomegranate, brass, smoke, citrus peel, tagine steam, the weight of a copper teapot. Confident, never modest, never apologetic. The hospitable energy of a Moroccan host expressed through bold sensory writing and imagery — not minimalism, not restraint.

Voice rules:
- Saturation, scale, weight, contrast are used deliberately, not muted away to look tasteful.
- Pricing, authority, and provenance are stated, not hedged.
- Every block of copy should be readable as one *moment*, not a feature row.

## Anti-references

What this site must never resemble:

- **Aggregator listicles**: Tripadvisor, GetYourGuide, Viator. The site must never read as a listing inside a marketplace.
- **Booking platforms**: OpenTable, Resy, SevenRooms. Too transactional, too platform-flavored, treats food as inventory.
- **Generic "Moroccan-themed" tourist kitsch**: over-saturated red-and-gold, busy mosaic patterns, lantern clipart, decorative-arabic display fonts, "exotic" cliché.
- **SaaS-tech minimalism**: white background, Inter or Geist, gradient hero, "AI-powered" anything. This brand is the opposite register.

## Design Principles

1. **Show, do not list.** Every section is one cinematic moment, not a row in a table. If a feature can be told as a sensory scene, tell it as a scene.
2. **Sensory language earns its place.** Copy uses smell, taste, heat, weight, color — not feature-bullet abstractions. "Souk-fresh tagine" beats "fresh ingredients." Adjectives that describe a feeling without describing a thing get cut.
3. **Confidence over caveats.** No "we strive to," no apologetic discounting. The price, the chef's authority, and the named experiences are stated.
4. **Premium through richness, not restraint.** This brand's luxury is saturation and warmth. Where another brand would mute, this one commits.
5. **Three languages, one voice.** EN, FR, and AR all read as native, with the same level of craft. Arabic is not a fallback translation; layout and typography respect RTL natively and use a hand-paired Arabic face, never an auto-substituted system fallback.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **WCAG AA** baseline.
- **Trilingual fluency.** EN, FR, AR each read as native, with correct linguistic register for the market. RTL Arabic typography is intentional (paired Arabic faces, mirrored layout where structurally appropriate), not automatic.
- **Performance is accessibility.** Target fast Time-to-Interactive on mobile networks in Morocco and on travelers' roaming connections, and snappy load on laptop. Hero video and large food imagery use modern formats, sensible preload, and lazy-load below the fold. Slow site = inaccessible site.
- **Motion respects user preference.** The cinematic, heightened motion vocabulary respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Type scale legible across the audience age range** (mid-30s to 50s+); body never below 16px, line length capped at 65–75ch on desktop.
